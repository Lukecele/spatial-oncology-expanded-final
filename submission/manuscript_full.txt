Abstract
Background: In early-stage EGFR-mutated non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), 36
months of adjuvant osimertinib significantly prolongs disease-free survival
(ADAURA trial). Longitudinal minimal residual disease data provide a clinical
context for studying recurrence after planned treatment discontinuation, but a
post-discontinuation fraction of events is not itself an estimate of hazard or proof
of biological rebound. The present work examines whether spatial transport
assumptions generate a testable explanation for residual disease; it does not infer
cytostatic dormancy from the clinical data.
Methods: We established a multi-scale biophysical reaction-diffusion framework
coupling Starling-Darcy fluid mechanics, Stokes-Einstein tissue transport, and
modified cylindrical Bessel functions of the second kind ( K 0) to define the spatial
limits of pharmacological penetration across tumor cords. The model uses a
provisional free fraction ( f u = 0.053), trough concentration (C trough = 540 nM ), in-vitro
IC50 (10.0 nM ), and explicitly assumed transport and clearance parameters.
Published cohorts were used as contextual benchmarks and plausibility checks,
not as independent clinical validation of the model. We also defined explicit
boundary conditions under which the one-dimensional model should fail.

Results: Under the baseline assumptions, the model gives D eff = 59.29 μ m / s , an
elimination rate k = 1.51× 10− 4 s−1, and penetration length λ = 627.6 μ m . The

the viable cylindrical cord volume. Sensitivity analysis is reported as a scenario
analysis rather than a complete uncertainty interval. These outputs are modelderived quantities, not direct measurements of drug concentration or residual
disease in human tumors. Adversarial stress-testing identified invasive margins,
the central nervous system, potency collapse, and low-shedding lesions as

Conclusions: The framework generates a testable hypothesis that tissue
transport and geometry may contribute to incomplete eradication of microscopic
EGFR-mutant disease. It defines a conditional sub-threshold compartment under
explicit model assumptions; it does not establish a causal sanctuary mechanism,
validate the CRI as a clinical score, or support treatment recommendations
without prospective testing.

Scope and evidentiary status
This manuscript contains three distinct components: (1) a mathematical reactiondiffusion model and its sensitivity analysis; (2) contextual comparison with
published human cohorts and pharmacology; and (3) a proposed prospective trial
concept. The first component is the primary contribution. The second is not
independent clinical validation, and the third is a protocol hypothesis rather than
an implemented or approved clinical trial. Claims are therefore phrased as model
outputs, contextual observations, or testable hypotheses.

Methods and computational verification
The index model was evaluated analytically from the Starling-Darcy and radial
reaction-diffusion equations described below. Parameter values were taken from
the cited pharmacokinetic, pharmacological, and interstitial-pressure sources
where available; assumptions that were not directly measured were labelled as
model inputs. The critical radius and conditional sub-threshold fraction were

recalculated over the stated parameter envelope rather than treated as clinical
observations.
Where accession-level records were available, the public-cohort analyses kept the
patient or matched pair as the unit of analysis. Some contextual cohorts were
identified from publications or study portals rather than downloaded into this
package. The CRI material is descriptive and the GIST benchmark is explicitly
exploratory; no threshold is presented as a validated clinical classifier, and no
treatment effect is inferred.
The included software_strumenti_calcolo/cri_calc.py script implements the
exploratory CRI specification using only the Python standard library. It maps the
declared histology, TP53/RB1 status, and stromal descriptor to the prespecified
component scores S , G , and M , then computes 0.47 S + 0.33 G + 0.20 M . Unknown
input categories are rejected rather than silently assigned a default. The script
returns component scores, the composite score, and an exploratory tier; it does
not estimate survival, issue treatment recommendations, or implement a clinical
decision rule.
The included calculator was checked with two focused tests and three reference
CLI cases; these checks establish implementation behaviour only, not calibration
or clinical validity. During the final cross-audit, the aligned translational
repository suite passed 7 tests and the expanded reaction-diffusion repository
suite passed 21 tests. These software checks do not establish clinical validity or
treatment efficacy.
The mathematical manuscript calculations are specified by the Markdown,
figures, derived reports, cited source parameters, and the included research-only
CRI calculator. Complete accession-linked data downloads are not included. The
repository also contains optional analysis dependencies for data-processing
workflows; therefore no claim of complete computational reproducibility is made
until those artifacts are restored and checked together.

1. Post-Adjuvant ADAURA Context and Spatial-Continuum
Hypothesis
The phase III ADAURA trial established 36 months of adjuvant osimertinib as a
standard treatment context for resected stage IB–IIIA EGFR-mutated (exon 19
deletion or L858R) non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) (Wu et al., 2020; Herbst et
al., 2023). The updated ADAURA report showed an overall-survival benefit

(Tsuboi et al., N Engl J Med 2023). In ADAURA, the 5-year disease-free survival
(DFS) rate in the osimertinib arm was approximately 73 %, with approximately
27 % of patients experiencing recurrence by that landmark.
The source-reported recurrence timing and ctDNA lead-time context are shown
in Figure 2; the figure is not an independent analysis of treatment efficacy.
Longitudinal minimal residual disease (MRD) tracking provides an unresolved
clinical context for recurrence after planned treatment discontinuation. In the
longitudinal circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) analysis of ADAURA (Herbst et al.,
Nature Medicine 2025, PMID: 40097663): 1. Post-cessation event timing: Among
patients who experienced disease recurrence, a reported fraction of events
occurred after the planned 36-month discontinuation of osimertinib; 2. Early
post-cessation clustering: The reported timing of some post-cessation
recurrences motivates prospective analysis but does not establish a hazard
increase; 3. ctDNA lead time: Molecular relapse preceded radiological
recurrence by a median lead time of 4.7 months (95 % CI, 2.2 – 5.6 months), with an
exploratory Cox hazard ratio for recurrence of 11.83 ( p < 0.0001).
Clinical Timeline & Month 36 research question:
• Months 0 – 36: Complete surgical R0 resection followed by 36 months of
adjuvant osimertinib (treatment exposure).
• Month 36 (The Cessation Crossroads): Planned drug withdrawal
under ADAURA protocol.
• Months 36 – 48: The reported event fraction after discontinuation and
its timing motivate prospective analysis; they do not by themselves
establish a hazard spike.
At month 36, treatment discontinuation and continuation represent clinically
distinct strategies whose comparative risks require prospective evidence; this
model does not recommend either strategy.
Conventional molecular oncology explains therapeutic failure through a purely
reductionist lens: secondary pocket mutations such as EGFR T790M for firstgeneration TKIs (Cross et al., 2014) or EGFR C797S after osimertinib (Thress et al.,
2015). Post-osimertinib progression can also involve unchanged EGFR mutant
clones, phenotypic transformations (small cell lung cancer [SCLC], epithelialmesenchymal transition [EMT]), or off-target bypass loops such as MET
amplification; the relative contribution of these mechanisms is not estimated in
this manuscript.

Here, we propose the Spatial-Genomic Oncology Continuum framework as a
testable model hypothesis: elevated interstitial fluid pressure and disorganized
microvascular architecture may contribute to a sub-therapeutic biophysical
compartment, consistent with established discussions of tumor interstitial
transport and drug penetration (Baxter and Jain, 1989; Minchinton and Tannock,
2006). The model does not establish that this mechanism causes relapse or that
residual cells remain cytostatically arrested during treatment.

2. Biophysical Pharmacology: Governing Model Equations
2.1 Starling-Darcy assumptions for convective transport
Fluid movement across the microvascular endothelium and interstitial matrix is
governed by Starling’s filtration law coupled to Darcy’s law:
J v = L p S [ ( P v − Pi ) − σ ( π v − π i ) )
v fluid = − K hyd ∇ P i

where L p is hydraulic permeability, S is surface area, P v is intravascular capillary
pressure, P i is interstitial fluid pressure (IFP), σ is the osmotic reflection
coefficient, and π v , π i are oncotic pressures.
In healthy alveolar lung tissue, functional lymphatics maintain negative
interstitial pressure ( P i ≈ − 2 mmHg ), driving continuous convective trans-vascular
flux.
In solid and acinar NSCLC, three mechanical factors disrupt this equilibrium: 1.
Solid stress from proliferating cancer cells compresses thin-walled intratumoral
lymphatic vessels, preventing fluid drainage; 2. Leaky, irregular neo-vessels cause
hyper-permeability to plasma proteins, elevating interstitial oncotic pressure (
π i → π v ); 3. Interstitial fluid pressure rises to match or approximate microvascular
hydrostatic pressure.
Direct manometric measurements in freshly resected human lung carcinomas
(Mori et al., Ann Thorac Cardiovasc Surg 2015, N = 211 patients analyzed, PMID:
25641031) report:

3. Cross-Drug Comparative Framework
The cross-drug numerical registry is withheld from the present results because
the required free fractions, tissue diffusivities, exposure definitions, payload-

release kinetics, and boundary conditions are not harmonized across agents.
Reporting r crit and conditional sub-threshold fractions without a verified
recalculation would create false precision. The osimertinib baseline is the only
numerical worked example retained here.
Conditional interpretation
The framework could be extended to other small molecules and antibody-drug
conjugates, but such comparisons require agent-specific measurements of free
concentration, tissue transport, binding, uptake, and payload release. No crossdrug efficacy ranking or sterilization claim is made in this manuscript.

5.4 The “Low-Shedder” ctDNA False-Negative Blind Spot
•

•

Where it fails: Indolent lepidic adenocarcinomas, mucinous subtypes, and

would not establish improved clinical sensitivity by itself.

5.5 Three proposed empirical tests
To make the framework testable, we state three laboratory or clinical
observations that could support, refine, or contradict its assumptions. They are
proposed experiments, not experiments performed in this manuscript:
1.

Gate 1 (Intra-vital MALDI-MSI): Measure free osimertinib concentrations
at > 150 μ m from CD31+ vessels and compare them with intravascular
concentrations. Results approaching 0.90 C 0 would challenge the assumed
diffusion-elimination decay.

2.

Gate 2 (ctDNA Lead Time Independence): Compare ctDNA lead times
across prospectively defined architectural groups. Similar lead times would
challenge the proposed coupling between conditional sub-threshold
geometry and recurrence kinetics.

3.

Gate 3 (Interventions on IFP): Test whether therapeutic normalization of
IFP in patient-derived xenografts changes measured surviving cord width.
A null result would challenge the assumed role of convective transport.

6. Exploratory Composite Resistance Index (CRI)
To formalize a testable post-adjuvant research hypothesis, we combine
histological pattern, TP53/RB1 status, and a qualitative stromal descriptor in an
exploratory, single-value research score. The current score has no time, ctDNA, or
treatment-duration input and is therefore not specific to month 36. It is not
calibrated, clinically validated, or intended for treatment decisions:
CRI = w S S + w G G + w M M

6.2 Reproducibility status
This package includes software_strumenti_calcolo/cri_calc.py, recovered from the

survival outputs. It is a deterministic research calculator, not a validated clinical
instrument. Smoke checks cover the component mapping and batch output;
independent validation of the score remains outstanding.

7. Conceptual SG-ADAURA design (not registered)
SG-ADAURA conceptual architecture:
1. Population: Patients with resected stage IB–IIIA EGFR-mutated NSCLC
in a documented post-adjuvant treatment state.
2. Score evaluation: The research score could be evaluated as a
prognostic or effect-modification variable only after its temporal inputs,
stromal-assessment rule, endpoint, and analysis plan are prospectively
specified. No treatment assignment, threshold, sample size, or
interventional arm is prescribed here.
3. Future study question: A separately registered protocol could

compare cessation and continuation strategies after clinical, ethical, and
statistical review; this manuscript does not specify those interventions.

8. Discussion: Implications and open questions for latergeneration TKIs
The oncology community has placed substantial hope in 4th-generation
reversible or allosteric EGFR TKIs (e.g. BLU-945, BDTX-1535) designed to target
EGFR C797S.
Later-generation TKIs may address selected biochemical resistance mechanisms,
but their spatial transport, target engagement, and clinical activity cannot be
inferred from the present model. A drug with similar assumed transport
parameters would motivate a sensitivity analysis, not a claim of identical
penetration, eradication, or mutation selection. Combining targeted therapy with
microenvironmental interventions is a testable research hypothesis, not a
solution established by this manuscript.

PART II. Computational Translation to Other Solid
Tumors
10. Rationale and Scope of the Expansion
The EGFR-mutated NSCLC model is the index system, not the endpoint of the
Spatial-Genomic Oncology Continuum. The same computational question can be
tested in other solid tumors treated with finite-duration targeted therapy:
Does tissue architecture, transport geometry, and treatment-state biology
identify a reproducible sub-therapeutic compartment that is invisible to
a purely pocket-level resistance model?
This expansion is deliberately computational and bioinformatic. It uses public
clinical, transcriptomic, spatial, single-cell, imaging, and pharmacological
resources. It does not claim new laboratory measurements, prospective
treatment recommendations, or clinical validation where those data are
unavailable.

12. Tumor-Specific Reparameterization
12.1 Melanoma
The lung cord geometry is replaced by a perivascular or shell geometry. The
computational inputs are BRAF/NRAS status, Breslow thickness, ulceration,
vascular density, immune exclusion, and a treatment-state label. The analysis
should compare pre-treatment and relapse/rechallenge states in public cohorts,
with patient rather than cell as the replication unit.
The repository registry records skcm_broad_brafresist_2012 (45 patients) and
GSE196434 (91 samples from 50 patients) as provenance-checked candidate
cohorts. Their treatment and mutation fields define a future, patient-level

analysis, but no melanoma inferential result is included in this package. The
repository therefore does not report an FDR, recurrence score, or treatment effect
for melanoma.
12.2 GIST
GIST is the first non-lung extension because its recurrence biology and adjuvant
imatinib duration are documented in public literature. Candidate inputs are
mitoses per 50 high-power fields, anatomical site, tumor rupture, size, KIT or
PDGFRA mutation group, and stromal context. The current repository contains a
transparent research score for these features.
The repository registry records gist_msk_2023 (438 complete patients and 205 RFS
events) and GSE225978 (21 high-risk patients) as validation resources. The current
repository calculation is a deterministic, uncalibrated research score
implemented in src/spatial_continuum_translational/gist.py; it does not output
recurrence probabilities or treatment recommendations. A separate exploratory
patient-level Cox benchmark, implemented in scripts/validate_gist_model.py,
reported HR 1.050 per cm for tumor size and HR 5.622 for metastatic status. A
five-fold internal benchmark reported mean C-index 0.881 (SD 0.036), with event
rates 10.3%, 35.6%, and 94.5% across low, middle, and high predicted-risk tertiles.
These results are not validation of the deterministic score: the model includes
metastatic status, a potentially leakage-prone covariate for recurrence, and the
derived report states that external validation is not available. They must
therefore be treated as internal exploratory diagnostics, not as a calibrated
clinical predictor or treatment rule.
12.3 Luminal Breast Cancer: Exploratory Benchmarking
The repository registry records brca_mskcc_2019 (54 response-annotated patients
and 14 progressive cases) as an exploratory treatment-response resource.
Therapy and mutation fields are incomplete, and no inferential mutation test or
calibrated CDK4/6 model is reported in this package.
An independent compatible treatment cohort is NEO-ORB (NCT01610284; PMID
30723140), a randomized phase II study of alpelisib plus letrozole versus placebo
plus letrozole in 257 HR-positive/HER2-negative patients. Its verified endpoints
are neoadjuvant objective response and pathological complete response. These
aggregate trial results support treatment-context replication, but they must not
be pooled with metastatic progression records, and patient-level validation of the
complete score remains pending.

12.4 Luminal Breast Cancer: Computational Extension
The model must replace the pulmonary cord with an anisotropic collagen-rich
matrix. Candidate inputs are stromal/CAF signatures, collagen organization,
Ki-67, endocrine receptor state, CDK4/6 exposure, and recurrence endpoint.
Spatial scores should be normalized within study before cross-study comparison.
12.5 Renal and Pancreatic Tumors
For clear-cell renal cancer, the central computational variables are hypoxia,
vascular normalization, sinusoidal architecture, and recurrence after a finite
adjuvant course. For pancreatic cancer, the priority variables are desmoplasia,
hyaluronan-associated restriction, KRAS/MAPK state, and immune exclusion. In
both systems, the model must allow tensorial diffusion rather than a fixed
isotropic coefficient.
The repository registry records the IMmotion150 renal cohort (263 patients, 262
PFS records, 79 progressions) and the PRINCE pancreatic cohort (74 patients, 73
PFS records, 12 progressions) as candidate treatment-response resources. Their
therapies and endpoints remain separate from EGFR, GIST, melanoma, and
breast analyses; no renal or pancreatic treatment-effect estimate is reported here.
The multi-tumor material in Part II is a hypothesis-generating extension, not a
completed validation study. It does not establish portability of the framework,
clinical utility, improved survival, replacement of clinician assessment, or a basis
for changing therapy.
